# Define an ideal customer profile

Use when a company needs to decide who to focus on, or to check that a message fits its audience.

1. **Collect evidence, not opinions.** List the customers who bought, renewed or referred, and the ones who churned or
   stalled. Take facts from the company's own records (supplied as company context); mark anything unverified as an assumption.
2. **Find the pattern.** For the best customers, note: the trigger that made them look, the job they hired you for, size and
   type of business, who decided, and what they used before. Keep only traits shared by most of them.
3. **Name the disqualifiers.** List traits that predict a bad fit (wrong size, no budget owner, no trigger). A profile that
   cannot exclude anyone is not a profile.
4. **Write the profile in one paragraph** a stranger could use to spot a fit: who, in what situation, wanting what outcome, and why now.
5. **Test it.** Draft one message for that profile and ask whether a real past customer would have recognised themselves. Revise.
6. **Record the result** in the company's Brain as a dated, sourced note. Revisit when evidence changes.

Output: ICP paragraph, 3 qualifiers, 3 disqualifiers, the list of assumptions still to verify.
